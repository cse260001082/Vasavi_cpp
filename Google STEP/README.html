Every sprint day

One day, four blocks

About 4 hours. Paper first, keyboard second. Keep the order; the minutes can bend.

  1. 45–60 min

    Dry-run on paper

    Draw a small array or a few nodes. Trace each loop and pointer move by hand before you write any code.

  2. 90–120 min

    Code and test in C++

    Type the solution in VS Code from scratch. Test the edge cases: an empty input, one element, negative numbers.

  3. 30–45 min

    Complexity and notes

    Work out the time and space cost, like O(1), O(N) or O(N log N). Write one takeaway in your notebook.

  4. 15 min

    Explain it out loud

    Pick one problem and explain it to your brother as if it were the real interview.

The 45-minute rule

Stuck on one problem for 45 minutes? Stop and ask for a hint. A hint plus 20 more minutes of your own work teaches more than two hours of staring at the screen.

The big picture

What is Google STEP?

STEP stands for Student Training in Engineering Program. It's Google's internship for 1st and 2nd-year undergraduates in computer science and related branches. Google runs it to find students early, a year or two before the usual internship season.

  • Who can apply1st and 2nd-year students in CSE, IT or a related branch
  • LengthAbout 10 to 12 weeks over the summer
  • Where in IndiaGoogle offices in Bangalore and Hyderabad
  • StipendPaid, and one of the best for 1st and 2nd-year interns
  • SelectionResume, an online coding test, then 2 technical interviews

Eligibility and dates change a little every year. Check the official STEP page before you apply.

Why it matters

STEP, then SWE intern, then a job offer

Each stage gives you a real shot at the next one. None of it is automatic, but STEP is the earliest way in.

  1. 1st or 2nd year

    Google STEP

    A 10 to 12 week summer project on a real Google team, with a host engineer and a co-intern.

  2. Pre-final year

    SWE intern

    STEP interns who do well are often invited back for a full software engineering internship, with a bigger piece of the project.

  3. Final year

    Pre-placement offer

    A strong SWE internship can end with a full-time offer before final-year placements even begin.

Why starting in semester 1 helps

  • If a pre-placement offer comes through, you skip the placement-season scramble.
  • Senior engineers review your code, so you learn how real production code gets written and reviewed.
  • Google on your resume helps with every internship, research group and fellowship after it.

What to expect

How selection works

  1. Step 1

    Apply online

    Your resume and college details.

  2. Step 2

    Online coding test

    1 or 2 problems on Google's own platform.

  3. Step 3

    2 technical interviews

    About 45 minutes each, usually one problem per round.

Five habits interviewers look for

  1. 1

    Think out loud

    Say your idea before you type. They're judging how you think, not only the final code. Your daily 15-minute explain block trains this.

  2. 2

    Brute force first

    State the simple solution and its cost, then improve it step by step.

  3. 3

    Ask about the input

    "Can the array be empty? Can numbers be negative?" Questions like these show you think like an engineer.

  4. 4

    Test by hand

    Trace a small example through your code before you say you're done.

  5. 5

    Stay calm when stuck

    Say "Let me reason through this" and write down what you already know. Silence worries interviewers more than slow progress.

  1. Aug–Sep

  2. Oct–Nov
    Online assessment1 or 2 coding problems, mostly on arrays and strings.
  3. Nov–Dec
    Technical interviews2 rounds of about 45 minutes. You code live in a shared doc or editor.
  4. Dec–Jan
    OffersSelected students get an offer letter.
  5. May–Jul
    Internship10 to 12 weeks at a Google office.
