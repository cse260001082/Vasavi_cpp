## 📌 What is Google STEP?

**STEP** stands for **Student Training in Engineering Program**. It is Google's internship program specifically designed for **1st-year and 2nd-year undergraduate students** in Computer Science (and related fields).

Google created STEP because they believe in investing early — they want to find talented students **before** the traditional internship cycle (which usually targets 3rd-year students) and give them a head start.

### Key Facts
| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| **Who can apply?** | 1st & 2nd year university students (CSE / IT / related) |
| **Duration** | ~10–12 weeks (summer internship) |
| **Location** | Google offices (Bangalore, Hyderabad for India) |
| **Stipend** | Highly competitive (among the best for freshman/sophomore internships) |
| **Selection** | Resume screening → Online coding test → 2 Technical Interviews |

## 🌟 Why Does STEP Matter?

### 1. **It's your earliest shot at Google**
Most companies don't even consider 1st/2nd year students. Google actively seeks them out through STEP. This is rare.

### 2. **The pipeline is real: STEP → Internship → PPO**
This is the golden path that many Googlers followed:

```
📘 1st/2nd Year          📗 3rd Year              📕 4th Year
┌──────────────┐    ┌──────────────────┐    ┌──────────────────┐
│  Google STEP │───→│ Google SWE Intern│───→│   PPO (Full-Time │
│  Internship  │    │   (Summer)       │    │   Software Eng.) │
└──────────────┘    └──────────────────┘    └──────────────────┘
```

- **STEP Intern** → You work on real Google projects with a host and a co-intern.
- **SWE Intern** (next year) → If you perform well in STEP, you get a **direct invite** for a full Software Engineering internship — no re-applying needed.
- **PPO (Pre-Placement Offer)** → Perform well in your SWE internship, and Google gives you a **full-time job offer before you even graduate**. You walk into final year with a Google offer in your pocket.

### 3. **The experience is transformative**
- You work on **production code** used by millions of people.
- You get a dedicated **mentor (host)** who is a senior Google engineer.
- You're paired with a **co-intern** so you're never alone.
- You attend tech talks, team events, and get full access to Google's campus.

### 4. **It opens every other door**
Even if you don't continue at Google, having "Google STEP Intern" on your resume makes you stand out for every other company, research program, and opportunity.

## 🎯 The Full Pipeline Explained

### Stage 1: Google STEP (1st / 2nd Year)

**What you do:**
- Work on a real engineering project for ~10 weeks
- Pair-program with a co-intern
- Get guided by a Google engineer (your "host")
- Present your project at the end

**What Google evaluates:**
- Your coding ability (can you write clean, working code?)
- Problem-solving skills (can you break down a problem?)
- Learning speed (can you pick up new things fast?)
- Collaboration (can you work well with your co-intern and team?)

### Stage 2: Google SWE Internship (Pre-final Year)

**If you do well in STEP, you're invited back** — often with a streamlined process (fewer or no interviews).

- More independence than STEP
- Larger, more complex projects
- You're treated more like a full-time engineer
- This is where PPO decisions are made

### Stage 3: PPO — Pre-Placement Offer (Final Year)

**If your SWE internship goes well:**
- Google extends a **full-time offer** before campus placements even begin
- You skip the stressful placement season entirely
- You start as a **Level 3 Software Engineer (L3)** at Google
- Competitive salary + stock (RSUs) + benefits from Day 1

### 📅 Phase 1: Arrays Mastery (Day 1 – Day 7)

#### Day 1: Array Fundamentals & Easy Warmup
*Building rock-solid array indexing and boundary tracking.*
- [ ] 1. **Largest Element in an Array** (Find max with linear scan)
- [ ] 2. **Second Largest Element in an Array without sorting** (Single pass tracking largest & 2nd largest)
- [ ] 3. **Check if the array is sorted** (Adjacent comparison $O(N)$)
- [ ] 4. **Remove duplicates from sorted array** (Two-pointer in-place removal)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Avoid sorting when a single pass ($O(N)$) can track state!

#### Day 2: Array Rotation & Zero Shifting
*Mastering in-place array manipulation without auxiliary memory.*
- [ ] 1. **Left Rotate an array by one place** (Shift left, place first element at end)
- [ ] 2. **Left rotate an array by D places** (The 3-reverse trick: reverse(0, d-1), reverse(d, n-1), reverse(0, n-1))
- [ ] 3. **Move Zeros to the end** (Two-pointer swap to preserve relative order of non-zeros)
- [ ] 4. **Linear Search** (Basic search & index return)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* The reverse algorithm rotates in $O(N)$ time and $O(1)$ space.

#### Day 3: Set Operations & Finding Unique
*Using two pointers on sorted arrays and clever bitwise arithmetic.*
- [ ] 1. **Find the Union of two sorted arrays** (Two pointers merge without duplicate insertion)
- [ ] 2. **Intersection of two sorted arrays** (Two pointers matching elements)
- [ ] 3. **Find the number that appears once, and other numbers twice** (Bitwise XOR: $a \oplus a = 0$)
- [ ] 4. **Find missing number in an array** (Sum formula $N(N+1)/2$ vs XOR approach)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* XOR is your secret weapon for finding unique elements.

#### Day 4: Subarrays & Two Sum Pattern
*Moving from single elements to continuous sequences.*
- [ ] 1. **Two Sum** (Variety 1: Yes/No with Two Pointers; Variety 2: Return indices with Hash Map)
- [ ] 2. **Longest Subarray with given sum K (Positives & Zeros)** (Sliding window technique)
- [ ] 3. **Longest Subarray with sum K (Positives + Negatives)** (Prefix Sum with Hash Map)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Sliding window works for positives; Prefix sum handles negatives!

#### Day 5: The "Famous Three" Interview Algorithms
*These 3 problems appear in almost every major tech interview.*
- [ ] 1. **Sort an array of 0s, 1s and 2s** (Dutch National Flag Algorithm: `low`, `mid`, `high`)
- [ ] 2. **Find the Majority Element (> N/2 times)** (Moore's Voting Algorithm)
- [ ] 3. **Kadane’s Algorithm** (Maximum Subarray Sum in $O(N)$)
- [ ] 4. **Print subarray with maximum subarray sum** (Extending Kadane's with tracking start & end indices)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Invariant pointers and candidate elimination make the impossible $O(N)$.

#### Day 6: Array Logic & State Tracking
*Tracking peaks, valleys, and leader elements.*
- [ ] 1. **Stock Buy and Sell** (Track minimum price seen so far and maximize profit)
- [ ] 2. **Rearrange the array in alternating positive and negative items** (Even/odd index placement)
- [ ] 3. **Leaders in an Array** (Scan from right to left tracking maximum)
- [ ] 4. **Longest Consecutive Sequence in an Array** (Using unordered set lookup $O(N)$)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Scanning from right to left often turns an $O(N^2)$ problem into $O(N)$.

#### Day 7: 2D Matrices — Vasavi's Home Turf!
*You already conquered rows, cols, and diagonals in `02_MatrixTraversing.cpp`!*
- [ ] 1. **Set Matrix Zeroes** (In-place marking using first row and first column)
- [ ] 2. **Rotate Matrix by 90 degrees** (Transpose matrix + reverse each row)
- [ ] 3. **Print the matrix in spiral manner (Spiral Matrix)** (Four-boundary approach: `top`, `bottom`, `left`, `right`)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Transpose + Reverse = 90° Clockwise Rotation. Boundary shrink handles spiral!

### 📅 Phase 2: Strings Mastery (Day 8 – Day 12)

#### Day 8: String Basics & Character Manipulation
*Understanding string immutability, ASCII arithmetic, and tokenization.*
- [ ] 1. **Remove Outermost Parentheses** (Track nesting balance depth)
- [ ] 2. **Reverse Words in a String** (Two pointers trimming and word extraction)
- [ ] 3. **Largest Odd Number in a String** (Scan from end for first odd digit)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Characters are just integers! `'5' - '0' == 5`.

#### Day 9: Patterns & Comparisons
*Comparing strings without brute-force quadratic checks.*
- [ ] 1. **Longest Common Prefix** (Sort & compare first with last OR vertical scan)
- [ ] 2. **Isomorphic Strings** (Dual character mapping `s -> t` and `t -> s`)
- [ ] 3. **Check Whether One String Is a Rotation of Another** (Check if $s_2$ is a substring of $s_1 + s_1$)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Concatenating $s_1 + s_1$ contains all possible rotations of $s_1$!

#### Day 10: Frequency Counting & Anagrams
*Using frequency buckets of size 26 for constant space $O(1)$.*
- [ ] 1. **Check if Two Strings are Anagram of Each Other** (Frequency array count == 0)
- [ ] 2. **Sort Characters by Frequency** (Hash map + bucket sort or priority queue)
- [ ] 3. **Maximum Nesting Depth of the Parentheses** (Count open vs closed brackets)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* An array of size 26 or 256 acts as an ultra-fast hash map.

#### Day 11: Conversions & Numerical Strings
*Parsing numbers and roman numerals.*
- [ ] 1. **Roman Number to Integer** (If smaller precedes larger, subtract; else add)
- [ ] 2. **Integer to Roman** (Greedy lookup table with values: 1000, 900, 500, 400...)
- [ ] 3. **Implement Atoi (String to Integer)** (Handle whitespace, +/- signs, digit conversion, overflow bounds)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Always check for integer overflow bounds (`INT_MAX` / `INT_MIN`) when parsing.

#### Day 12: Substrings & Palindromes
*The art of symmetry and contiguous segments.*
- [ ] 1. **Check if String is Palindrome** (Two pointers meeting in middle)
- [ ] 2. **Longest Palindromic Substring** (Expand around center for both odd and even lengths)
- [ ] 3. **Count Number of Substrings with K Distinct Characters** (Sliding window `atMost(K) - atMost(K-1)`)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Expand around center avoids DP table overhead and runs in $O(1)$ space.

### 📅 Phase 3: Linked Lists (Day 13 – Day 18)

#### Day 13: Demystifying Pointers & The Linked Node
*Moving beyond arrays to dynamic heap memory.*
- [ ] 1. **Introduction to Linked List & Struct Node Creation** (Define `struct Node { int data; Node* next; }`)
- [ ] 2. **Traversal in a Linked List** (Iterating until `temp == nullptr`)
- [ ] 3. **Find the Length of a Linked List** (Iterative count)
- [ ] 4. **Search an Element in a Linked List** (Return boolean/index)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* A node is just data + a pointer to the next memory box!

#### Day 14: Insertion & Deletion Operations
*Mastering edge cases: empty list, head manipulation, tail manipulation.*
- [ ] 1. **Insertion at Head of LL** (New node points to head; update head)
- [ ] 2. **Insertion at Tail & K-th Position** (Traverse to $(k-1)$-th node, rewire links)
- [ ] 3. **Deletion of Head & Tail of LL** (Properly `free`/`delete` old memory)
- [ ] 4. **Deletion of K-th Node & Node with Value X** (Rewire previous node's `next`)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Always check if `head == nullptr` or deleting the head node first!

#### Day 15: Doubly Linked Lists (DLL)
*Bi-directional navigation with `prev` and `next` pointers.*
- [ ] 1. **Introduction to Doubly Linked List & Representation** (Node with `prev`, `data`, `next`)
- [ ] 2. **Insert and Delete in Doubly Linked List** (Remember to update both directions!)
- [ ] 3. **Reverse a Doubly Linked List** (Swap `next` and `prev` pointers for all nodes)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* When updating a DLL node, 4 pointer links change instead of 2.

#### Day 16: The Tortoise & Hare (Fast & Slow Pointers)
*Floyd's algorithm for loop detection and midpoint finding.*
- [ ] 1. **Middle of a Linked List** (`slow` moves 1 step, `fast` moves 2 steps)
- [ ] 2. **Detect a Loop/Cycle in a Linked List** (If `fast == slow`, a cycle exists)
- [ ] 3. **Find the Starting Point of a Loop** (Reset one pointer to head; both move 1 step)
- [ ] 4. **Length of a Loop in a Linked List** (Fix one pointer and count steps around cycle)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Fast & Slow pointers solve cycle problems in $O(1)$ auxiliary space.

#### Day 17: Structural Reversals & Reordering
*Core interview patterns asked in almost every tech round.*
- [ ] 1. **Reverse a Linked List (Iterative & Recursive)** (3-pointer: `prev`, `curr`, `next`)
- [ ] 2. **Check if a Linked List is a Palindrome** (Find mid, reverse 2nd half, compare)
- [ ] 3. **Odd Even Linked List** (Group odd indices followed by even indices)
- [ ] 4. **Remove the Nth Node from the End of List** (Advance fast by N steps, then move both)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Drawing the pointers on paper first makes reversals effortless.

#### Day 18: List Arithmetic & Merge Operations
*Simulating math directly on linked nodes.*
- [ ] 1. **Add 1 to a number represented as a Linked List** (Recursion / reverse list carry propagation)
- [ ] 2. **Add two numbers represented as Linked Lists** (Dummy head pattern + carry accumulator)
- [ ] 3. **Intersection Point of Two Y-shaped Linked Lists** (Length difference shifting OR dual pointer cycle)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* A `DummyNode` simplifies edge cases so you never have to special-case the head.

#### Day 19: LIFO & FIFO Foundations
*Understanding Last-In-First-Out and First-In-First-Out mechanics.*
- [ ] 1. **Implement Stack using Arrays** (`top` index pointer, `push`, `pop`, `peek`)
- [ ] 2. **Implement Queue using Arrays** (`front`, `rear`, circular buffer modulo `% N`)
- [ ] 3. **Implement Stack using Queue** (Using single queue with size rotation)
- [ ] 4. **Implement Queue using Stack** (Amortized $O(1)$ using `input` and `output` stacks)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Stack = stack of plates (LIFO); Queue = cafeteria line (FIFO).

#### Day 20: Real-World Applications & Memory
*Dynamic sizes, bracket validation, and constant time minimum tracking.*
- [ ] 1. **Implement Stack using Linked List** (Insert/delete at head in $O(1)$)
- [ ] 2. **Implement Queue using Linked List** (Insert at tail, delete at head)
- [ ] 3. **Check for Balanced Parentheses** (Push open brackets; pop and match on closing brackets)
- [ ] 4. **Implement Min Stack** (Track minimum element in $O(1)$ time with auxiliary stack or $2x - \text{min}$ formula)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Balanced parentheses is Google's all-time favorite screening question!

#### Day 21: Monotonic Stack — The Secret Superpower
*The single most tested advanced stack concept in tech interviews.*
- [ ] 1. **Next Greater Element I (NGE)** (Right-to-left scan maintaining decreasing stack)
- [ ] 2. **Next Greater Element II (Circular Array)** (Hypothetical $2N$ array traversal modulo `% N`)
- [ ] 3. **Next Smaller Element** (Monotonic increasing stack)
- [ ] 4. **Number of NGEs to the right** (Query-based counting)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Monotonic stack reduces $O(N^2)$ nested loops down to clean $O(N)$!

#### Day 22: Problem Solving with Monotonic Stack
*Physical simulations using monotonic order.*
- [ ] 1. **Trapping Rain Water** (Two pointer approach OR monotonic stack boundary calculation)
- [ ] 2. **Asteroid Collision** (Stack simulation of moving positive and negative rocks)
- [ ] 3. **Stock Span Problem** (Consecutive smaller or equal days using monotonic stack)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Trapping rain water checks `min(leftMax, rightMax) - height[i]`.

#### Day 23: Grand Challenges of Stacks
*Tackling Google-level hard interview problems with calm confidence.*
- [ ] 1. **Largest Rectangle in a Histogram** (Find Previous Smaller & Next Smaller for every bar)
- [ ] 2. **Maximal Rectangles in Binary Matrix** (Convert each row into a histogram problem!)
- [ ] 3. **Sliding Window Maximum** (Monotonic deque storing indices in decreasing order)
- 💡 *Daily Takeaway:* Maximal rectangle in a matrix is just the histogram algorithm run on every row!

### 📝 The Interview Process

1. **Online Application** — Resume + university details
2. **Online Coding Assessment** — 1–2 coding problems (usually on a Google platform)
3. **Technical Interviews** — 2 rounds, ~45 min each
   - Each interview: 1 coding problem
   - You code in a Google Doc (no autocomplete!)
   - Interviewer evaluates: correctness, code quality, communication, optimization

### 💡 Interview Tips
- **Think out loud** — Explain your approach before you code
- **Start with brute force** — Then optimize
- **Test your code** — Walk through examples manually
- **Ask clarifying questions** — Shows maturity
- **Be honest** — If you're stuck, say so and explain what you're thinking
