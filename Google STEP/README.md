# 🚀 Google STEP — Your Roadmap from 1st Year to Google

## 📌 What is Google STEP?

**STEP** stands for **Student Training in Engineering Program**. It is Google's internship program specifically designed for **1st-year and 2nd-year undergraduate students** in Computer Science (and related fields).

Google created STEP because they believe in investing early — they want to find talented students **before** the traditional internship cycle (which usually targets 3rd-year students) and give them a head start.

### Key Facts
| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| **Who can apply?** | 1st & 2nd year university students (CSE / IT / related) |
| **Duration** | ~10–12 weeks (summer internship) |
| **Location** | Google offices (Bangalore, Hyderabad for India) |
| **Stipend** | Highly competitive (among the best for freshman/sophomore internships) |
| **Selection** | Resume screening → Online coding test → 2 Technical Interviews |

## 🌟 Why Does STEP Matter?

### 1. **It's your earliest shot at Google**
Most companies don't even consider 1st/2nd year students. Google actively seeks them out through STEP. This is rare.

### 2. **The pipeline is real: STEP → Internship → PPO**
This is the golden path that many Googlers followed:

```
📘 1st/2nd Year          📗 3rd Year              📕 4th Year
┌──────────────┐    ┌──────────────────┐    ┌──────────────────┐
│  Google STEP │───→│ Google SWE Intern│───→│   PPO (Full-Time │
│  Internship  │    │   (Summer)       │    │   Software Eng.) │
└──────────────┘    └──────────────────┘    └──────────────────┘
```

- **STEP Intern** → You work on real Google projects with a host and a co-intern.
- **SWE Intern** (next year) → If you perform well in STEP, you get a **direct invite** for a full Software Engineering internship — no re-applying needed.
- **PPO (Pre-Placement Offer)** → Perform well in your SWE internship, and Google gives you a **full-time job offer before you even graduate**. You walk into final year with a Google offer in your pocket.

### 3. **The experience is transformative**
- You work on **production code** used by millions of people.
- You get a dedicated **mentor (host)** who is a senior Google engineer.
- You're paired with a **co-intern** so you're never alone.
- You attend tech talks, team events, and get full access to Google's campus.

### 4. **It opens every other door**
Even if you don't continue at Google, having "Google STEP Intern" on your resume makes you stand out for every other company, research program, and opportunity.

## 🎯 The Full Pipeline Explained

### Stage 1: Google STEP (1st / 2nd Year)

**What you do:**
- Work on a real engineering project for ~10 weeks
- Pair-program with a co-intern
- Get guided by a Google engineer (your "host")
- Present your project at the end

**What Google evaluates:**
- Your coding ability (can you write clean, working code?)
- Problem-solving skills (can you break down a problem?)
- Learning speed (can you pick up new things fast?)
- Collaboration (can you work well with your co-intern and team?)

### Stage 2: Google SWE Internship (Pre-final Year)

**If you do well in STEP, you're invited back** — often with a streamlined process (fewer or no interviews).

- More independence than STEP
- Larger, more complex projects
- You're treated more like a full-time engineer
- This is where PPO decisions are made

### Stage 3: PPO — Pre-Placement Offer (Final Year)

**If your SWE internship goes well:**
- Google extends a **full-time offer** before campus placements even begin
- You skip the stressful placement season entirely
- You start as a **Level 3 Software Engineer (L3)** at Google
- Competitive salary + stock (RSUs) + benefits from Day 1

## 📚 How to Prepare (Starting NOW)

### 🔧 Technical Skills to Build

#### Semester 1–2 (Where you are now!)
- [x] **Learn C/C++ well** — You're doing this right now! Keep practicing.
- [ ] **Understand basic data structures** — Arrays, Strings, Linked Lists, Stacks, Queues
- [ ] **Start simple coding problems** — HackerRank (Easy), Codeforces (Div 3/4)
- [ ] **Learn time complexity basics** — What makes code fast or slow (Big-O)

#### Semester 3–4
- [ ] **Data Structures & Algorithms** — Trees, Graphs, Hashmaps, Heaps, Sorting
- [ ] **Practice on LeetCode** — Start with Easy, move to Medium
- [ ] **Learn Python** — Many people prefer it for interviews (shorter to write)
- [ ] **Build 1–2 small projects** — A web app, a tool, anything you can show

#### Before Applying
- [ ] **Solve 150+ LeetCode problems** (mix of Easy & Medium)
- [ ] **Practice mock interviews** — With friends or platforms like Pramp
- [ ] **Prepare your resume** — Highlight projects, coursework, and competitions

### 📝 The Interview Process

1. **Online Application** — Resume + university details
2. **Online Coding Assessment** — 1–2 coding problems (usually on a Google platform)
3. **Technical Interviews** — 2 rounds, ~45 min each
   - Each interview: 1 coding problem
   - You code in a Google Doc (no autocomplete!)
   - Interviewer evaluates: correctness, code quality, communication, optimization

### 💡 Interview Tips
- **Think out loud** — Explain your approach before you code
- **Start with brute force** — Then optimize
- **Test your code** — Walk through examples manually
- **Ask clarifying questions** — Shows maturity
- **Be honest** — If you're stuck, say so and explain what you're thinking
